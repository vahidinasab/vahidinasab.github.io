Clean energy job

Solar panel installer

Checks roofs and fits solar panels on them. Works with electricians to connect home batteries.

  • Installs
  • Maintains and repairs

Pay

£25,000 to £47,000 a year

From starting pay to experienced. This is the typical yearly pay on the job's official profile.

A day might include

Checking a roof is strong enough, fixing panels in place, and showing a family how much power they are making.

Skills you use

  • Working safely at height
  • Teamwork
  • Careful measuring

It could suit you if you enjoy

  • Making and fixing things
  • Being outdoors and active
  • Nature and the climate

GCSE topics it uses

From the Department for Education's subject content for each GCSE in England. Exam boards add detail, so your course may cover more.

Science

  • Energy resources
    Choosing between wind, solar, hydro and nuclear power, and knowing which are renewable.
  • Mains electricity and safety
    Wiring a home charger, solar system or heat pump so that it is safe.
  • Batteries and the mains
    Home batteries and solar panels work with direct current while the mains is alternating current, so an inverter converts between the two.

Maths

  • Percentages and growth
    Working out what solar panels could save, or how a cost changes over time.

Design and technology

  • Energy generation and storage
    Choosing a solar panel and battery to power a product.
  • Your design project
    Half of your marks come from designing and making a prototype. Exam boards set new contexts each year, and where one allows an energy theme you could design something like a solar-powered light.

Electronics

  • Diodes and rectification
    Turning alternating current from the mains into direct current to charge a battery.
  • Your system project
    You design and build your own electronic system. A circuit that switches on a small load only when a light sensor detects sunshine works like a simple solar power diverter.

Engineering

  • Energy, maintenance and end of life
    Planning the servicing of a wind farm, and what happens to old panels and batteries.
  • Testing under load
    Making sure turbine blades and roof fixings are strong enough.

Ways in

  • Apprenticeship: Building Services Engineering foundation apprenticeship (level 2), open to 16 to 21-year-olds with no set qualifications
  • Apprenticeship: Installation and Maintenance Electrician (level 3)
  • T Level: Building Services Engineering for Construction

What these words mean

Apprenticeship
A real job with training. You are paid, learn at work and study part of the time, often at a college. You can start from age 16.
Foundation apprenticeship
A first step into a type of work for 16 to 21-year-olds. You do not need set grades to start.
T Level
A two-year course after GCSEs, worth the same as three A levels. It includes at least 45 days working with an employer.
Levels 2 to 7
Level 2 is the same standard as GCSEs at grades 4 to 9. Level 3 is the same as A levels and T Levels. Levels 4 and 5 are between A levels and a degree. Level 6 is a bachelor's degree, and level 7 a master's degree.
