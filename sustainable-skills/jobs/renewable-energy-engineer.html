Clean energy job

Renewable energy engineer

Plans and designs wind, solar and other clean energy projects. Might check if a place suits a wind farm, or make solar panels work better.

  • Designs and plans

Pay

£31,000 to £61,000 a year

From starting pay to experienced. This is the typical yearly pay on the job's official profile.

A day might include

Studying wind or sunlight data for a site, using design software, and meeting the team that will build the project.

Skills you use

  • Maths and physics
  • Computer modelling
  • Planning projects

It could suit you if you enjoy

  • Science and how things work
  • Nature and the climate
  • Maths, numbers and data

GCSE topics it uses

From the Department for Education's subject content for each GCSE in England. Exam boards add detail, so your course may cover more.

Science

  • Energy resources
    Choosing between wind, solar, hydro and nuclear power, and knowing which are renewable.
  • The National Grid
    How power gets from a wind farm to your street, and why substations are needed.
  • Power and efficiency
    Comparing a heat pump with a gas boiler, and finding where a home wastes energy.
  • Climate change
    The reason the world is switching to clean energy.
  • Life cycle assessment
    Judging the impact of a solar panel or battery, from making it to recycling it.
  • Generators and transformers Separate physics only
    How a wind turbine makes electricity, and how substations change the voltage.
  • Cells and fuel cells Separate chemistry only
    How batteries and hydrogen fuel cells store and release energy.

Maths

  • Scatter graphs and predictions
    Spotting the link between cold weather and energy use, then making a prediction.

Geography

  • Energy and resources
    Where our energy comes from, and how places plan for wind farms, solar farms and new power lines.
  • Climate change
    The evidence behind the move to clean energy.

Design and technology

  • Energy generation and storage
    Choosing a solar panel and battery to power a product.
  • Your design project
    Half of your marks come from designing and making a prototype. Exam boards set new contexts each year, and where one allows an energy theme you could design something like a solar-powered light.

Engineering

  • Energy, maintenance and end of life
    Planning the servicing of a wind farm, and what happens to old panels and batteries.
  • Engineering and the environment
    Weighing up the environmental gains and costs of an energy project.

Economics

  • Externalities
    How carbon taxes and clean energy subsidies work.

Ways in

  • Degree: Energy engineering, environmental engineering, or renewable or sustainable energy
  • Entry: Usually 2 or 3 A levels, including maths and a science
What do these words mean?
A level
A two-year course in one subject after GCSEs, at a sixth form or college. Many university courses ask for A levels.
Degree
A university course, usually three years if you study full time.
