Clean energy job

Electronics engineer

Designs and tests circuits and electronic systems. In clean energy, that means the electronics inside chargers, batteries and solar power systems.

  • Designs and plans

Pay

£28,000 to £60,000 a year

From starting pay to experienced. This is the typical yearly pay on the job's official profile.

A day might include

Designing a circuit on a computer, building and testing a prototype, and working out why it does not work yet.

Skills you use

  • Circuit design
  • Coding
  • Testing and problem solving

It could suit you if you enjoy

  • Gadgets, electronics and coding
  • Science and how things work
  • Maths, numbers and data

GCSE topics it uses

From the Department for Education's subject content for each GCSE in England. Exam boards add detail, so your course may cover more.

Science

  • Batteries and the mains
    Home batteries and solar panels work with direct current while the mains is alternating current, so an inverter converts between the two.
  • Electric motors
    How the motor in an electric car turns the wheels.
  • Life cycle assessment
    Judging the impact of a solar panel or battery, from making it to recycling it.
  • Cells and fuel cells Separate chemistry only
    How batteries and hydrogen fuel cells store and release energy.

Maths

  • Graphs and rates of change
    Reading how fast a battery charges, or how quickly demand for power rises in the evening.

Design and technology

  • Energy generation and storage
    Choosing a solar panel and battery to power a product.
  • Electronic systems and smart controls
    The sensors and controls in smart heating and smart chargers.
  • Programmable components
    The programmable parts inside smart thermostats, plugs, meters and chargers.
  • Life cycle assessment
    Picking materials with a smaller footprint, from mining to recycling.
  • Your design project
    Half of your marks come from designing and making a prototype. Exam boards set new contexts each year, and where one allows an energy theme you could design something like a solar-powered light.

Computer science

  • Programming
    Writing software that decides when a smart charger or heat pump should switch on.
  • Embedded systems
    The small computers inside chargers, heat pumps and solar inverters.
  • Cyber security
    Keeping connected chargers and energy systems safe from attack.

Electronics

  • Sensing, processing and output
    How a charger senses that a car is plugged in, or a heat pump reads the temperature.
  • Switching circuits
    Switching power on and off safely, which chargers and solar inverters do all the time.
  • Diodes and rectification
    Turning alternating current from the mains into direct current to charge a battery.
  • Microcontrollers
    Programming the small computer at the heart of a smart device.
  • Your system project
    You design and build your own electronic system. A circuit that switches on a small load only when a light sensor detects sunshine works like a simple solar power diverter.

Engineering

  • Programmable control systems
    The control systems that keep wind turbines and heat pumps running.

Ways in

  • Degree: Electronic engineering, or electronic and electrical engineering
  • Degree apprenticeship: Embedded Electronic Systems Design and Development Engineer (level 6)
  • Work: Start as an electronics engineering technician and train on the job
What do these words mean?
Apprenticeship
A real job with training. You are paid, learn at work and study part of the time, often at a college. You can start from age 16.
Degree apprenticeship
An apprenticeship that ends with a university degree. You earn a wage and do not pay course fees.
Levels 2 to 7
Level 2 is the same standard as GCSEs at grades 4 to 9. Level 3 is the same as A levels and T Levels. Levels 4 and 5 are between A levels and a degree. Level 6 is a bachelor's degree, and level 7 a master's degree.
Degree
A university course, usually three years if you study full time.
