Clean energy job

Electrician

Fits and fixes the wiring, sockets and lights in homes and other buildings. Many also fit solar panels and car chargers.

  • Installs
  • Maintains and repairs

Pay

£26,000 to £45,000 a year

From starting pay to experienced. This is the typical yearly pay on the job's official profile.

A day might include

Wiring a new kitchen, fitting a car charger on a driveway, and testing circuits to check they are safe.

Skills you use

  • Working safely with electricity
  • Solving problems
  • Explaining things to customers

It could suit you if you enjoy

  • Making and fixing things
  • Gadgets, electronics and coding
  • Business and money
  • Helping people

GCSE topics it uses

From the Department for Education's subject content for each GCSE in England. Exam boards add detail, so your course may cover more.

Science

  • Mains electricity and safety
    Wiring a home charger, solar system or heat pump so that it is safe.
  • Power ratings of appliances
    Reading a smart meter display and spotting which appliances use the most energy.
  • Batteries and the mains
    Home batteries and solar panels work with direct current while the mains is alternating current, so an inverter converts between the two.

Maths

  • Rates and unit pricing
    Comparing electricity prices in pence per kilowatt-hour, and working out what a charge costs.

Design and technology

  • Electronic systems and smart controls
    The sensors and controls in smart heating and smart chargers.

Electronics

  • Switching circuits
    Switching power on and off safely, which chargers and solar inverters do all the time.

Business

  • Starting a business
    Many electricians and mechanics go on to run their own firms.
  • Cash flow
    Paying for equipment before the customer pays you.

Ways in

  • T Level: Building Services Engineering for Construction (electrotechnical engineering)
  • Apprenticeship: Installation and Maintenance Electrician (level 3)
  • Apprenticeship: Domestic Electrician (level 3)

What these words mean

Apprenticeship
A real job with training. You are paid, learn at work and study part of the time, often at a college. You can start from age 16.
T Level
A two-year course after GCSEs, worth the same as three A levels. It includes at least 45 days working with an employer.
Levels 2 to 7
Level 2 is the same standard as GCSEs at grades 4 to 9. Level 3 is the same as A levels and T Levels. Levels 4 and 5 are between A levels and a degree. Level 6 is a bachelor's degree, and level 7 a master's degree.
