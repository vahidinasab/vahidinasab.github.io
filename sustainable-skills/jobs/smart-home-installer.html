Clean energy job

Smart home installer

Plans, fits and sets up smart lighting, heating, security and TV systems in homes, so they all work together.

  • Installs
  • Designs and plans

Pay

£24,000 to £55,000 a year

From starting pay to experienced. This is the typical yearly pay on the job's official profile.

A day might include

Installing smart heating controls, setting up lights and speakers to work together, and fixing a Wi-Fi problem.

Skills you use

  • Technology and networks
  • Wiring
  • Explaining technology simply

It could suit you if you enjoy

  • Gadgets, electronics and coding
  • Making and fixing things
  • Helping people

GCSE topics it uses

From the Department for Education's subject content for each GCSE in England. Exam boards add detail, so your course may cover more.

Science

  • Power ratings of appliances
    Reading a smart meter display and spotting which appliances use the most energy.

Design and technology

  • Electronic systems and smart controls
    The sensors and controls in smart heating and smart chargers.
  • Programmable components
    The programmable parts inside smart thermostats, plugs, meters and chargers.

Computer science

  • Embedded systems
    The small computers inside chargers, heat pumps and solar inverters.
  • Cyber security
    Keeping connected chargers and energy systems safe from attack.
  • Networks
    How smart meters, thermostats and chargers connect to each other and to the internet.

Electronics

  • Sensing, processing and output
    How a charger senses that a car is plugged in, or a heat pump reads the temperature.
  • Microcontrollers
    Programming the small computer at the heart of a smart device.

Ways in

  • Apprenticeship: Smart Home Technician (level 3)
  • Apprenticeship: Electrical, Electronic Product Service and Installation Engineer (level 3)
  • T Level: Building Services Engineering for Construction

What these words mean

Apprenticeship
A real job with training. You are paid, learn at work and study part of the time, often at a college. You can start from age 16.
T Level
A two-year course after GCSEs, worth the same as three A levels. It includes at least 45 days working with an employer.
Levels 2 to 7
Level 2 is the same standard as GCSEs at grades 4 to 9. Level 3 is the same as A levels and T Levels. Levels 4 and 5 are between A levels and a degree. Level 6 is a bachelor's degree, and level 7 a master's degree.
