Roadmap / Clean energy jobs

For pupils in Years 7 to 11 and their families

Clean energy jobs

19 jobs that install, run and repair clean, local energy. Pick one to see its pay, the GCSE topics it uses and the ways in.

  • Electrician

    • Installs
    • Maintains and repairs

    £26,000 to £45,000 a year

    Fits and fixes the wiring, sockets and lights in homes and other buildings. Many also fit solar panels and car chargers.

  • Solar panel installer

    • Installs
    • Maintains and repairs

    £25,000 to £47,000 a year

    Checks roofs and fits solar panels on them. Works with electricians to connect home batteries.

  • Heat pump engineer

    • Installs
    • Maintains and repairs

    £24,000 to £46,000 a year

    Fits, checks and fixes heat pumps and other low carbon heating. Shows people how to use their new heating.

  • Electric vehicle technician

    • Maintains and repairs

    £22,000 to £42,000 a year

    Finds and fixes faults in cars and vans, including the high-voltage batteries in hybrid and electric cars.

  • Wind turbine technician

    • Installs
    • Maintains and repairs

    £25,000 to £47,000 a year

    Builds, checks and fixes wind turbines on land or out at sea. Often works outdoors and high up.

  • Electricity network worker

    • Installs
    • Maintains and repairs

    £23,000 to £42,000 a year

    Builds and looks after power lines, underground cables and substations. Connects new things, like solar panels, to the grid.

  • Domestic energy assessor

    • Advises

    £24,000 to £35,000 a year

    Visits homes to check the insulation, heating and lighting. Then gives the home an energy rating from A to G.

  • Renewable energy engineer

    • Designs and plans

    £31,000 to £61,000 a year

    Plans and designs wind, solar and other clean energy projects. Might check if a place suits a wind farm, or make solar panels work better.

  • Electronics engineer

    • Designs and plans

    £28,000 to £60,000 a year

    Designs and tests circuits and electronic systems. In clean energy, that means the electronics inside chargers, batteries and solar power systems.

  • Energy data scientist

    • Works with data

    £32,000 to £83,000 a year

    Uses data and computer models to spot patterns. In energy, this helps predict how much power people will need, and the best time to charge electric cars.

  • Sustainability practitioner

    • Advises

    £24,000 to £50,000 a year

    Helps a business or council do less harm to the environment. Makes a plan, gets people on board and checks how it is going.

  • Plumber

    • Installs
    • Maintains and repairs

    £24,000 to £46,000 a year

    Fits and fixes water pipes, heating and drains in homes and businesses. This includes heat pumps and solar hot water.

  • Refrigeration and air-conditioning installer

    • Installs
    • Maintains and repairs

    £25,000 to £45,000 a year

    Fits, checks and fixes cooling and air conditioning in buildings, cold stores and fridge lorries. The main apprenticeship also covers heat pumps.

  • Building services engineer

    • Designs and plans
    • Maintains and repairs

    £28,000 to £51,000 a year

    Plans the heating, lighting, power and fresh air in big buildings like offices and hospitals, and the controls that run them.

  • Smart meter installer

    • Installs
    • Advises

    £24,000 to £38,000 a year

    Fits smart gas and electricity meters in homes and businesses. Checks they are safe and shows people how to use them to save energy.

  • Smart home installer

    • Installs
    • Designs and plans

    £24,000 to £55,000 a year

    Plans, fits and sets up smart lighting, heating, security and TV systems in homes, so they all work together.

  • Cavity insulation installer

    • Installs

    £24,000 to £40,000 a year

    Fits insulation in walls, lofts and ceilings, including in older homes, so buildings stay warm using less energy.

  • Electrical engineering technician

    • Installs
    • Maintains and repairs

    £24,000 to £48,000 a year

    Fits, tests and fixes electrical equipment and control systems, including in wind turbines and heat pumps.

  • Engineering maintenance technician

    • Maintains and repairs

    £27,000 to £48,000 a year

    Keeps machines and equipment running. Checks them often, finds faults and fixes them.

Pay is for a year, from starting pay to experienced. It is the typical yearly pay on each job's official profile.

Use this to explore, then talk to your careers adviser before you choose. Courses, grades and pay can change. Information checked October 2026.
