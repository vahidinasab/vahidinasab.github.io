Clean energy job

Refrigeration and air-conditioning installer

Fits, checks and fixes cooling and air conditioning in buildings, cold stores and fridge lorries. The main apprenticeship also covers heat pumps.

  • Installs
  • Maintains and repairs

Pay

£25,000 to £45,000 a year

From starting pay to experienced. This is the typical yearly pay on the job's official profile.

A day might include

Servicing the cooling in a supermarket, fitting air conditioning in an office, and testing a heat pump for leaks.

Skills you use

  • Handling refrigerants safely
  • Electrical skills
  • Finding faults

It could suit you if you enjoy

  • Making and fixing things
  • Science and how things work

GCSE topics it uses

From the Department for Education's subject content for each GCSE in England. Exam boards add detail, so your course may cover more.

Science

  • Heating and specific heat capacity
    Working out how much energy a heat pump has to supply to warm the water and the rooms in a home.
  • Changes of state
    Inside a heat pump, a refrigerant evaporates and condenses over and over to carry heat from the outside air into the home.

Ways in

  • Apprenticeship: Refrigeration, Air Conditioning and Heat Pump Engineering (level 3)
  • Apprenticeship: Building Services Engineering Installer (level 2)
  • T Level: Building Services Engineering for Construction

What these words mean

Apprenticeship
A real job with training. You are paid, learn at work and study part of the time, often at a college. You can start from age 16.
T Level
A two-year course after GCSEs, worth the same as three A levels. It includes at least 45 days working with an employer.
Levels 2 to 7
Level 2 is the same standard as GCSEs at grades 4 to 9. Level 3 is the same as A levels and T Levels. Levels 4 and 5 are between A levels and a degree. Level 6 is a bachelor's degree, and level 7 a master's degree.
