Clean energy job

Sustainability practitioner

Helps a business or council do less harm to the environment. Makes a plan, gets people on board and checks how it is going.

  • Advises

Pay

£24,000 to £50,000 a year

From starting pay to experienced. This is the typical yearly pay on the job's official profile. Official job profile: corporate responsibility and sustainability practitioner.

A day might include

Measuring how much energy an organisation uses, running a meeting about a new plan, and writing a progress report.

Skills you use

  • Persuading people
  • Planning
  • Working with numbers

It could suit you if you enjoy

  • Nature and the climate
  • Business and money
  • Helping people

GCSE topics it uses

From the Department for Education's subject content for each GCSE in England. Exam boards add detail, so your course may cover more.

Science

  • Climate change
    The reason the world is switching to clean energy.
  • Life cycle assessment
    Judging the impact of a solar panel or battery, from making it to recycling it.

Maths

  • Percentages and growth
    Working out what solar panels could save, or how a cost changes over time.

Geography

  • Energy and resources
    Where our energy comes from, and how places plan for wind farms, solar farms and new power lines.
  • Climate change
    The evidence behind the move to clean energy.

Design and technology

  • Life cycle assessment
    Picking materials with a smaller footprint, from mining to recycling.

Computer science

  • Technology and the environment
    How much energy devices and data centres use, and who can see the data a smart meter collects.

Engineering

  • Engineering and the environment
    Weighing up the environmental gains and costs of an energy project.

Business

  • Environmental influences
    Why firms are cutting their emissions and switching to clean energy.

Economics

  • Supply, demand and price
    Why some electricity tariffs charge more at 6pm than at 2am.
  • Externalities
    How carbon taxes and clean energy subsidies work.
  • Sustainability
    Weighing up who pays for the move to clean energy, and who gains.

Ways in

  • Degree: Environment and sustainability, energy and engineering, economics, or business management
  • Apprenticeship: Corporate Responsibility and Sustainability Practitioner (level 4)
  • Degree apprenticeship: Sustainability Business Specialist (level 7)
What do these words mean?
Apprenticeship
A real job with training. You are paid, learn at work and study part of the time, often at a college. You can start from age 16.
Degree apprenticeship
An apprenticeship that ends with a university degree. You earn a wage and do not pay course fees.
Levels 2 to 7
Level 2 is the same standard as GCSEs at grades 4 to 9. Level 3 is the same as A levels and T Levels. Levels 4 and 5 are between A levels and a degree. Level 6 is a bachelor's degree, and level 7 a master's degree.
Degree
A university course, usually three years if you study full time.
