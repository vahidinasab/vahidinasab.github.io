Clean energy job

Electricity network worker

Builds and looks after power lines, underground cables and substations. Connects new things, like solar panels, to the grid.

  • Installs
  • Maintains and repairs

Pay

£23,000 to £42,000 a year

From starting pay to experienced. This is the typical yearly pay on the job's official profile. Official job profile: electricity distribution worker.

A day might include

Joining underground cables, working on a substation, and connecting a new solar farm or charger to the grid.

Skills you use

  • Working safely with high voltage
  • Teamwork
  • Working outdoors in all weathers

It could suit you if you enjoy

  • Being outdoors and active
  • Making and fixing things
  • Science and how things work

GCSE topics it uses

From the Department for Education's subject content for each GCSE in England. Exam boards add detail, so your course may cover more.

Science

  • The National Grid
    How power gets from a wind farm to your street, and why substations are needed.
  • Generators and transformers Separate physics only
    How a wind turbine makes electricity, and how substations change the voltage.

Ways in

  • Apprenticeship: Power Industry Overhead Linesperson, Substation Fitter or Distribution Cable Jointer (level 3)
  • Apprenticeship: Electrical Power Networks Engineer (level 4)
  • College: Engineering or electrical engineering courses
What do these words mean?
Apprenticeship
A real job with training. You are paid, learn at work and study part of the time, often at a college. You can start from age 16.
Levels 2 to 7
Level 2 is the same standard as GCSEs at grades 4 to 9. Level 3 is the same as A levels and T Levels. Levels 4 and 5 are between A levels and a degree. Level 6 is a bachelor's degree, and level 7 a master's degree.
College
A further education college. It runs courses for 16 to 19-year-olds and adults, including T Levels and apprenticeship training.
