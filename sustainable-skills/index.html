          What to do next

            For parents and carers
            • Ask the school's careers leader which local employers work in sustainable energy, and whether they offer work experience.
            • Ask the school about careers guidance, and use the official careers website for England (formerly the National Careers Service) or the careers service where you live.
            • Talk through the jobs in this roadmap together. Many start with an apprenticeship, and a few usually need a degree.

            Good to know

            • In England you must stay in education or training until 18: at college or sixth form, in an apprenticeship, or working or volunteering 20 hours or more a week alongside part-time study. School leaving age
            • If you finish Year 11 without at least a grade 4 in English or maths, you keep studying it after 16. Maths and English rules
            • T Levels are two-year courses that include at least 45 days working with an employer. 16 to 19 study programmes
            • V Levels start in September 2027. Each is the size of one A level, so you can mix them with A levels. The first are in digital, education and early years, and finance and accounting. V Levels explained
            • Vocational GCSEs are planned in some subjects from as early as September 2029, and will replace today's technical awards. Department for Education announcement

            Official careers advice

            Talk your choices through with an adviser

            This roadmap is a guide, not advice. Start with your school or college careers adviser; these free official services can help too. The GCSE topics here come from England's subject content; Wales and Northern Ireland have their own GCSE courses, and Scotland has National qualifications.
