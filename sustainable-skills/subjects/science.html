Science GCSE and clean energy jobs

14 GCSE topics to look out for, and the 17 clean energy jobs that use them.

From the Department for Education's subject content for each GCSE in England. Exam boards add detail, so your course may cover more. Everyone takes English, maths and science.

GCSE topics to look out for

  • Energy resources

    Choosing between wind, solar, hydro and nuclear power, and knowing which are renewable.

    What the course says (official wording)“describe the main energy sources available for use on Earth (including fossil fuels, nuclear fuel, bio-fuel, wind, hydro-electricity, the tides and the Sun)”

    Used by Renewable energy engineer Wind turbine technician Solar panel installer

  • The National Grid

    How power gets from a wind farm to your street, and why substations are needed.

    What the course says (official wording)“in the national grid, electrical power is transferred at high voltages from power stations”

    Used by Electricity network worker Renewable energy engineer Wind turbine technician Electrical engineering technician

  • Mains electricity and safety

    Wiring a home charger, solar system or heat pump so that it is safe.

    What the course says (official wording)“recall the differences in function between the live, neutral and earth mains wires”

    Used by Electrician Solar panel installer Heat pump engineer Smart meter installer Electrical engineering technician

  • Power and efficiency

    Comparing a heat pump with a gas boiler, and finding where a home wastes energy.

    What the course says (official wording)“calculate energy efficiency for any energy transfer, and describe ways to increase efficiency”

    Used by Heat pump engineer Domestic energy assessor Renewable energy engineer Building services engineer

  • Insulation

    Why insulating a home cuts heating bills and helps a heat pump work well.

    What the course says (official wording)“explain ways of reducing unwanted energy transfer e.g. through lubrication, thermal insulation; describe the effects, on the rate of cooling of a building, of thickness and thermal conductivity of its walls (qualitative only)”

    Used by Domestic energy assessor Heat pump engineer Cavity insulation installer Building services engineer

  • Heating and specific heat capacity

    Working out how much energy a heat pump has to supply to warm the water and the rooms in a home.

    What the course says (official wording)“describe how heating a system will change the energy stored within the system and raise its temperature or produce changes of state”“define the term specific heat capacity and distinguish between it and the term specific latent heat”

    Used by Heat pump engineer Plumber Building services engineer Refrigeration and air-conditioning installer

  • Changes of state

    Inside a heat pump, a refrigerant evaporates and condenses over and over to carry heat from the outside air into the home.

    What the course says (official wording)“describe how, when substances melt, freeze, evaporate, condense or sublimate, mass is conserved, but that these physical changes differ from chemical changes because the material recovers its original properties if the change is reversed”

    Used by Heat pump engineer Refrigeration and air-conditioning installer

  • Power ratings of appliances

    Reading a smart meter display and spotting which appliances use the most energy.

    What the course says (official wording)“describe, with examples, the relationship between the power ratings for domestic electrical appliances and the changes in stored energy when they are in use”

    Used by Smart meter installer Domestic energy assessor Smart home installer Electrician

  • Batteries and the mains

    Home batteries and solar panels work with direct current while the mains is alternating current, so an inverter converts between the two.

    What the course says (official wording)“describe how, in different domestic devices, energy is transferred from batteries and the a.c. mains to the energy of motors or of heating devices”“recall that the domestic supply in the UK is a.c., at 50Hz and about 230 volts, explain the difference between direct and alternating voltage”

    Used by Electrician Solar panel installer Electrical engineering technician Electronics engineer

  • Electric motors

    How the motor in an electric car turns the wheels.

    What the course says (official wording)“explain how this force is used to cause rotation in electric motors”

    Used by Electric vehicle technician Electronics engineer Electrical engineering technician

  • Climate change

    The reason the world is switching to clean energy.

    What the course says (official wording)“describe the potential effects of increased levels of carbon dioxide and methane on the Earth’s climate”“how these effects may be mitigated, including consideration of scale, risk and environmental implications”

    Used by Sustainability practitioner Renewable energy engineer

  • Life cycle assessment

    Judging the impact of a solar panel or battery, from making it to recycling it.

    What the course says (official wording)“interpret data from a life-cycle assessment of a material or product”

    Used by Sustainability practitioner Renewable energy engineer Electronics engineer

  • Generators and transformers Separate physics only

    How a wind turbine makes electricity, and how substations change the voltage.

    What the course says (official wording)“explain how this effect is used in an alternator to generate a.c., and in a dynamo to generate d.c.”“explain how the effect of an alternating current in one circuit in inducing a current in another is used in transformers”

    Used by Wind turbine technician Electricity network worker Renewable energy engineer

  • Cells and fuel cells Separate chemistry only

    How batteries and hydrogen fuel cells store and release energy.

    What the course says (official wording)“recall that a chemical cell produces a potential difference until the reactants are used up”“evaluate the advantages and disadvantages of hydrogen/oxygen and other fuel cells for given uses”

    Used by Electric vehicle technician Renewable energy engineer Electronics engineer

Source: Combined science GCSE subject content and Biology, chemistry and physics GCSE subject content (Department for Education)
