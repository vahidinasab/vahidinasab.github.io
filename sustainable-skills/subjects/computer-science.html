Computer science GCSE and clean energy jobs

5 GCSE topics to look out for, and the 6 clean energy jobs that use them.

From the Department for Education's subject content for each GCSE in England. Exam boards add detail, so your course may cover more.

GCSE topics to look out for

  • Programming

    Writing software that decides when a smart charger or heat pump should switch on.

    What the course says (official wording)“design, write, test and refine programs, using one or more high-level programming language”

    Used by Energy data scientist Electronics engineer

  • Embedded systems

    The small computers inside chargers, heat pumps and solar inverters.

    What the course says (official wording)“hardware components and embedded systems”

    Used by Electronics engineer Electric vehicle technician Smart home installer

  • Cyber security

    Keeping connected chargers and energy systems safe from attack.

    What the course says (official wording)“ways to protect software systems (during design, creation, testing, and use)”

    Used by Energy data scientist Electronics engineer Smart home installer

  • Networks

    How smart meters, thermostats and chargers connect to each other and to the internet.

    What the course says (official wording)“connectivity, both wired and wireless”

    This is one of the network topics every course must cover.

    Used by Smart home installer Smart meter installer Energy data scientist

  • Technology and the environment

    How much energy devices and data centres use, and who can see the data a smart meter collects.

    What the course says (official wording)“the ethical, legal and environmental impacts of digital technology on wider society, including issues of privacy and cyber security”

    Used by Energy data scientist Sustainability practitioner

Source: Computer science GCSE subject content (Department for Education)
